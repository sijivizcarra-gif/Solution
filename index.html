CHAPTER 4

PRESENTATION, ANALYSIS, AND INTERPRETATION OF DATA

This chapter presents the results of the study on the relationship between community support network and the level of maternal stress during pregnancy among 30 pregnant women in Barangay Salunayan. The data were analyzed using frequency, percentage, mean, standard deviation, and Pearson Product-Moment Correlation Coefficient. The results are presented in tabular form followed by analysis, interpretation, and literature support.

Table 4.1 shows the demographic profile of the 30 pregnant women respondents in Barangay Salunayan. The majority of respondents or 40.0% are 30 years old and above, followed by 33.3% belonging to the 20-24 years old age bracket, and 26.7% are 25-29 years old. This indicates that pregnant women in the community come from various age groups with the older age group comprising the largest portion.

In terms of marital status, the majority or 70.0% are married, 23.3% are single, and 6.7% are separated. This suggests that most respondents have spousal support during pregnancy, while a notable portion are single or separated who may have different support needs.

Regarding educational attainment, 36.7% are college graduates, 30.0% are high school graduates, 23.3% reached high school level, 6.7% are college level, and 3.3% are elementary graduate. This indicates that most respondents have attained at least high school education, with a significant number being college graduates.

For trimester of pregnancy, 6.7% are in their first trimester, 13.3% in second trimester, and 16.7% in third trimester. However, 63.3% of respondents have no data reported for this variable, which limits the analysis of stress levels across different stages of pregnancy.

In terms of monthly family income, the majority or 73.3% have a monthly income of ₱3,000 to ₱5,000, while 26.7% have ₱6,000 to ₱10,000. None of the respondents fall under the higher income brackets. This indicates that most respondents belong to low-income families, which may affect their financial capacity to access maternal healthcare and other pregnancy-related needs.

These findings are supported by the World Health Organization (2022) which reports that pregnant women from different age groups face varying challenges during pregnancy. Leach et al. (2018) found that married pregnant women typically have better access to emotional and practical support from their partners, which serves as a protective factor against stress. Cutrona & Gardner (2021) noted that educational attainment influences health literacy and the ability to navigate healthcare systems, affecting maternal stress levels. Raghuveer et al. (2023) identified low income as a significant predictor of financial stress during pregnancy, which compounds maternal anxiety. Glover (2016) explained that stress levels may vary across different trimesters due to physical discomfort and anticipation of childbirth.

Table 4.2 presents the community support network available to pregnant women in Barangay Salunayan. The overall mean of 3.66 indicates a High level of community support, suggesting that pregnant women generally receive adequate support from their community.

Among the three types of support, Emotional Support received the highest average mean of 3.94 which is interpreted as High. The indicator "I receive encouragement from my family during pregnancy" obtained the highest mean of 4.23 which is Very High, showing that family members play a crucial role in providing emotional comfort to expectant mothers. "My partner shows care and understanding" received a mean of 4.10 which is High, and "I feel loved and accepted by people around me" received 4.07 which is also High. These findings indicate that emotional support from family and partners is strongly present in the community.

Informational Support obtained an average mean of 3.83 which is High, with "Health workers provide clear information about pregnancy" receiving the highest rating of 4.17. This indicates that healthcare providers in Barangay Salunayan effectively communicate important pregnancy-related information to mothers. "I know where to go when I have pregnancy concerns" received 4.03 and "I am informed about danger signs during pregnancy" received 3.97, both interpreted as High. However, "The barangay conducts seminars about maternal care" received a moderate rating of 3.07, suggesting a need for more organized community-based maternal education programs.

Practical/Instrumental Support received the lowest average mean of 3.21 which is Moderate. "Someone accompanies me to prenatal check-ups" received a mean of 3.73 which is High, and "There is available transportation to the health center" received 3.43 which is also High. However, "I receive financial help for medical expenses" with a mean of 2.87 and "The health center has complete medicines and supplies" with a mean of 2.73 both received moderate ratings, indicating that financial and material support are areas needing improvement. "Someone helps me with household chores" also received a moderate rating of 3.27.

These findings are supported by Bedaso et al. (2021) who emphasized that emotional support from family provides comfort and reassurance, which is essential for maternal mental health during pregnancy. Taylor (2020) found that family support serves as the primary buffer against stress for pregnant women, particularly in collectivist cultures. Shorey & Chan (2020) noted that clear communication from healthcare providers reduces uncertainty and anxiety during pregnancy. The World Health Organization (2022) recommends regular community-based maternal education programs to ensure all pregnant women receive essential health information. Abdi et al. (2022) identified instrumental support such as financial help, transportation, and household assistance as critical but often lacking in many communities. Rini et al. (2019) found that inadequate medical supplies in health facilities increase stress among pregnant women who worry about accessing necessary care.

These findings align with Glover (2016) who identified fear of childbirth as a major source of anxiety for pregnant women, particularly those in their first pregnancy. (2021) reported that concerns about fetal health are universal among pregnant women and contribute significantly to maternal stress. Rini et al. (2019) explained that body image concerns and physical discomfort during pregnancy can negatively impact mental health. Leach et al. (2018) emphasized that relationship quality with partners is a key determinant of maternal stress levels during pregnancy. Phiri et al. (2021) documented similar high stress levels among pregnant women, particularly those facing multiple stressors simultaneously.

Table 4.5 presents the Pearson correlation analysis between community support network and maternal stress among 30 pregnant women in Barangay Salunayan. The Pearson correlation coefficient between community support network and maternal stress is 0.246, indicating a weak positive relationship. This means that there is a slight tendency for maternal stress to increase as community support increases, although the relationship is weak. The p-value of 0.1899 is greater than 0.05, suggesting that this relationship is not statistically significant.

Among the three types of support, Practical Support showed the strongest correlation with maternal stress at 0.278, followed by Informational Support at 0.215 and Emotional Support at 0.198. All three show weak positive relationships and are not statistically significant with p-values greater than 0.05. This indicates that none of the individual support types have a significant relationship with maternal stress levels.

The correlation between monthly family income and maternal stress is 0.356 which is a moderate positive relationship with a p-value of 0.048, and this is statistically significant at the 0.05 level. This indicates that respondents with lower monthly income reported higher stress levels, confirming that economic factors play a significant role in maternal stress during pregnancy.

These findings are supported by Abdi et al. (2022) who found that while community support reduces stress, its effect size varies across contexts and may be moderated by other factors. Raghuveer et al. (2023) identified practical support such as financial help and transportation as most directly related to stress reduction. Erbil (2024) found that economic factors account for significant variance in pregnancy-related stress, with the finding that monthly family income shows a statistically significant relationship with maternal stress at 0.356 with a p-value of 0.048. Shorey & Chan (2020) noted that perception of support alone does not guarantee stress reduction and that actual support quality matters more.

4.6 SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

The findings of this study reveal that pregnant women in Barangay Salunayan come from diverse demographic backgrounds. The majority are 30 years old and above (40.0%), married (70.0%), college graduates (36.7%), and belong to low-income families earning ₱3,000 to ₱5,000 monthly (73.3%). A significant portion of respondents (63.3%) have no data reported for trimester of pregnancy.

In terms of community support, respondents experience a High level of community support with an overall mean of 3.66. Emotional Support received the highest rating at 3.94, particularly family encouragement which rated Very High at 4.23. Informational Support also rated High at 3.83, with health workers providing clear information rating highest at 4.17. Practical Support received the lowest rating at 3.21 which is Moderate, with financial help for medical expenses (2.87) and health center supplies (2.73) being the areas needing most improvement.

The respondents experience a High level of maternal stress with an overall mean of 3.52. The highest stressors are worry about labor and delivery pain (4.10), financial expenses for delivery (4.03), and anxiety about baby's health (3.77). Relationship concerns and emotional changes rated as Moderate stressors.

In terms of perceived adequacy and quality of support, respondents reported a High level of satisfaction with an overall mean of 3.51. Information clarity (3.93), family support meeting expectations (3.90), and respectful health workers (3.87) received the highest ratings. Unfair treatment in the health center rated Low at 2.20, indicating that most respondents do not experience discrimination.

The Pearson correlation analysis showed that the relationship between community support network and maternal stress is weak positive at 0.246 and not statistically significant with a p-value of 0.1899. Among the three support types, Practical Support showed the strongest correlation at 0.278, followed by Informational Support at 0.215 and Emotional Support at 0.198, all not statistically significant. However, monthly family income showed a moderate positive and statistically significant relationship with maternal stress at 0.356 with a p-value of 0.048, indicating that respondents with lower income experience higher stress levels.

These findings indicate that while community support networks exist at a high level in Barangay Salunayan, they do not significantly correlate with maternal stress. Instead, economic factors as shown by monthly family income emerge as a more critical determinant of stress during pregnancy. This suggests that financial capacity may have a stronger influence on maternal well-being than community support alone.
